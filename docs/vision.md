# Home Decoration Website — Vision & Creative Direction

## 1. Project Overview

This website is for a premium home-decoration/interior-decoration brand.

The goal is **not** to create another generic business website that simply lists services such as furniture, curtains, wallpapers, lighting, or decor.

The website should function as a **visual experience** that communicates what the brand does through imagery, motion, atmosphere, storytelling, and carefully controlled typography.

A first-time visitor should understand within a few seconds:

> **This brand transforms spaces into beautiful, intentional homes.**

The website should feel closer to a premium interior-design editorial, architecture portfolio, or luxury lifestyle experience than a conventional local-business website.

The design must feel modern, sophisticated, cinematic, intentional, and memorable.

---

# 2. Core Concept

## "A space should feel like you."

The central idea behind the website is:

**We don't simply decorate rooms. We transform spaces into places that feel complete, personal, and worth living in.**

The website should communicate this without relying on large amounts of explanatory text.

The primary storytelling medium should be:

- Cinematic imagery
- Video
- Motion
- Transitions
- Typography
- Spatial composition
- Before/after transformation
- Materials and details
- Atmosphere

Text should support the experience rather than dominate it.

---

# 3. The Story We Want to Tell

The entire website should feel like a visual journey.

The basic narrative is:

**An empty space has potential.**

↓

**Design gives it character.**

↓

**Materials give it texture.**

↓

**Lighting gives it atmosphere.**

↓

**Furniture gives it life.**

↓

**Details make it personal.**

↓

**The space becomes home.**

This progression should influence the structure and transitions throughout the website.

The visitor should feel like they are witnessing the transformation of a space rather than scrolling through a collection of business sections.

---

# 4. First Impression / Hero Section

The hero is the most important part of the website.

It should immediately establish:

1. What the company does
2. The quality level of the brand
3. The visual language of the website
4. The emotional tone

The hero should be highly visual.

A cinematic video is strongly preferred if suitable footage is available.

The video should not simply be a random luxury-interior montage.

Ideally, it should communicate transformation.

Possible visual progression:

**Empty/minimal room**
→ **walls and textures**
→ **lighting**
→ **curtains**
→ **furniture**
→ **decor**
→ **finished space**

The final frame should feel warm, complete, aspirational, and lived-in.

The visitor should understand the business even if they do not read the headline.

### Hero principle

The visual should answer:

> "What do these people actually do?"

before the text has to explain it.

Possible headline direction:

**YOUR SPACE.  
YOUR STORY.**

or

**A SPACE SHOULD  
FEEL LIKE YOU.**

Supporting copy should remain short.

Example direction:

> Thoughtfully designed spaces, shaped around the way you live.

CTA direction:

**EXPLORE THE SPACES**

The exact copy can change later.

The important thing is the emotional and visual intention.

---

# 5. Hero Motion Philosophy

Motion should feel cinematic and purposeful.

Avoid:

- Random bouncing animations
- Excessive parallax
- Generic fade-ins everywhere
- Overly complicated 3D effects
- Excessive particle effects
- Animation for the sake of animation

Prefer:

- Slow camera-like movement
- Image reveals
- Mask transitions
- Elegant text entrances
- Layered movement
- Scroll-driven transformations
- Subtle depth
- Smooth image scaling
- Controlled transitions between sections

The website should feel expensive because of **restraint**, not because it contains every animation technique available.

---

# 6. Overall Visual Language

The visual identity should be:

- Premium
- Architectural
- Editorial
- Cinematic
- Warm
- Modern
- Sophisticated
- Minimal
- Slightly dramatic
- Image-driven

Avoid making the website look like:

- A SaaS website
- A generic template
- A restaurant website
- A standard e-commerce store
- A basic local-business website
- A collection of rounded cards
- A typical "AI-generated website"

The design should have a strong point of view.

---

# 7. Color Direction

The primary visual direction should be dark and sophisticated.

Potential palette:

- Deep charcoal
- Near-black
- Warm ivory
- Soft beige
- Muted bronze
- Natural wood tones
- Deep muted green where appropriate

Colors should feel inspired by:

- Interior materials
- Wood
- Stone
- Metal
- Warm lighting
- Fabric
- Architecture

Avoid overly saturated colors.

Gold should not be overused.

The website should never look like a stereotypical "luxury gold and black" template.

The palette should feel **natural and architectural**, not flashy.

---

# 8. Typography

Typography should be a major part of the visual identity.

Use a strong editorial hierarchy.

The primary display typography can have an elegant character, while supporting text should remain extremely clean and readable.

Large headlines should have enough space around them to feel intentional.

Typography should communicate confidence without requiring heavy decorative styling.

Avoid:

- Too many fonts
- Excessive font weights
- Huge paragraphs
- Decorative typography everywhere
- Generic startup-style typography

Typography should work together with photography.

---

# 9. Navigation

The navigation should remain minimal.

The visitor should never feel like they are navigating a dashboard.

Possible structure:

**Logo / Brand**
**Work**
**Services**
**About**
**Contact**

The navigation can be transparent or integrated into the hero.

A compact menu interaction may be used if it improves the visual experience.

The navigation should never compete with the hero.

---

# 10. "The Space Begins" Section

After the hero, introduce the idea of transformation.

The visitor encounters an empty or minimal space.

Possible headline:

> **Every room starts with a blank canvas.**

The section should establish that the design process begins before decoration.

The visual should create curiosity about what the space will become.

This section should transition naturally into the transformation experience.

---

# 11. Transformation Section

This should be one of the signature sections of the website.

Instead of showing a list of services, visually demonstrate how a space comes together.

The room can progressively transform as the user scrolls.

Possible sequence:

### 01 — THE WALL

Texture, paint, wallpaper, architectural surfaces.

### 02 — THE LIGHT

Pendant lights, chandeliers, ambient lighting, natural light.

### 03 — THE FABRIC

Curtains, upholstery, cushions, soft materials.

### 04 — THE FORM

Furniture and spatial arrangement.

### 05 — THE DETAILS

Decorative objects, artwork, plants, accessories.

### 06 — THE SPACE

The completed room.

The user should feel that each layer contributes to the final environment.

This should be visual rather than a traditional numbered service list.

---

# 12. Sell Feelings Before Products

The website should avoid immediately presenting products as the primary value proposition.

Instead of:

- Curtains
- Furniture
- Lighting
- Wallpapers
- Decor

lead with experiences and moods.

Potential categories:

### CALM

Soft textures. Warm light. Neutral tones.

### BOLD

Contrast. Statement pieces. Dramatic lighting.

### TIMELESS

Balanced proportions. Natural materials. Classic forms.

### MODERN

Clean lines. Architectural forms. Minimal composition.

These categories are examples of direction, not fixed content.

The goal is to make visitors think:

> "I want my home to feel like this."

rather than:

> "They sell curtains."

---

# 13. Portfolio

The portfolio should be one of the strongest sections.

Do not use a generic grid of small cards.

Avoid:

```text
[Image] [Image] [Image]
Project 1
Project 2
Project 3
```

Instead, use an editorial presentation.

Large imagery should dominate the viewport.

Each project can include minimal information:

**RESIDENCE 04**

Living / Dining / Bedroom

Location

The photography should be the primary content.

Projects should feel like pages from an architecture or interior-design magazine.

Possible interactions:

- Horizontal movement
- Vertical image reveals
- Image expansion
- Scroll-driven transitions
- Full-screen project views
- Layered typography

Motion must remain elegant and performant.

---

# 14. Details / Materials Section

After showing complete spaces, zoom into the details.

Show close-up imagery of:

- Fabric
- Wood
- Stone
- Metal
- Lighting
- Handles
- Curtains
- Cushions
- Decorative objects
- Wall textures
- Shadows
- Architectural details

The concept:

> **Luxury lives in the details.**

This section should communicate craftsmanship and attention to detail.

The visitor should understand that the final feeling of a room comes from hundreds of small decisions.

---

# 15. Human / Emotional Layer

The website should eventually move from:

**beautiful spaces**

to:

**why those spaces matter.**

The final experience should communicate that a home isn't only about appearance.

It is about:

- Comfort
- Identity
- Memories
- Relaxation
- Hosting people
- Everyday rituals
- Feeling at home

A possible emotional statement:

> **Because home isn't about how a room looks.  
> It's about how it makes you feel.**

The exact wording is flexible.

The emotional idea is not.

---

# 16. Final CTA

The final CTA should feel like the natural conclusion of the story.

After seeing the transformation, the user should be invited to begin their own.

Possible direction:

> **Let's create your space.**

CTA:

**START YOUR SPACE**

or:

**BOOK A CONSULTATION**

The CTA should feel confident and simple.

Avoid aggressive sales language.

---

# 17. Footer

The footer should remain visually consistent with the rest of the website.

It should contain necessary information without becoming cluttered.

Possible content:

- Brand
- Navigation
- Contact
- Social links
- Location
- Copyright
- Final brand statement

The footer should feel like the final page of the visual story rather than a generic website footer.

---

# 18. Image Direction

Photography is extremely important.

Images should preferably feature:

- Strong composition
- Natural lighting
- Architectural lines
- Material textures
- Warm atmosphere
- Realistic interiors
- Cinematic framing
- Human-scale spaces
- Detail shots

Avoid generic stock photography whenever possible.

Do not use random images simply because they look attractive.

Images must support the story.

The visual language should remain consistent across the entire website.

---

# 19. Video Direction

Video can be used heavily, especially in the hero and transformation sections.

Videos should be:

- Short
- Cinematic
- High quality
- Carefully cropped
- Optimized for web
- Visually meaningful

Video should never exist merely because "modern websites use video."

Every video must contribute to storytelling.

For mobile, provide an appropriate crop or fallback when necessary.

Performance is important.

Large video files should not destroy load times.

---

# 20. Scroll Experience

Scrolling should feel intentional.

The website can use:

- Sticky visual sections
- Scroll-triggered reveals
- Image transformations
- Pinning
- Horizontal galleries
- Layered transitions
- Progressive text reveals
- Smooth section transitions

However:

**Do not make the website difficult to use just to make it look impressive.**

The experience should remain intuitive.

The user should always understand where they are.

---

# 21. Spatial Design

Whitespace is important.

Do not fill every pixel.

Allow:

- Images to breathe
- Typography to breathe
- Sections to have visual rhythm
- Large compositions to dominate
- Negative space to create luxury

The design should alternate between:

**dense visual moments**

and

**quiet moments.**

This creates pacing.

The website should feel like a film, not a slideshow of components.

---

# 22. Responsive Design

The experience must be designed for:

- Desktop
- Laptop
- Tablet
- Mobile

Mobile should not simply be the desktop design stacked vertically.

The storytelling must be intentionally adapted for smaller screens.

For example:

- Desktop multi-column compositions may become focused single-image compositions.
- Horizontal interactions may become vertical sequences.
- Large videos must be cropped intelligently.
- Typography must maintain hierarchy without overwhelming the screen.
- Navigation should become simpler.

The mobile experience must remain premium.

---

# 23. Performance

Visual quality must not come at the cost of terrible performance.

Prioritize:

- Optimized images
- Proper image formats
- Lazy loading where appropriate
- Compressed videos
- Responsive image sizes
- Efficient animations
- Minimal unnecessary JavaScript
- Avoiding excessive third-party libraries

Animations should preferably use performant browser properties.

The website should feel smooth rather than heavy.

---

# 24. Accessibility

Even though the site is highly visual, accessibility remains important.

Maintain:

- Readable contrast
- Proper semantic HTML
- Keyboard accessibility
- Accessible navigation
- Meaningful alt text
- Reduced-motion consideration
- Proper heading hierarchy
- Usable mobile interactions

The premium experience should not come at the expense of usability.

---

# 25. Design References

The provided reference UI is a source of inspiration for:

- Premium dark visual language
- Editorial composition
- Large typography
- Image strips
- Minimal navigation
- Architectural feeling
- Strong visual hierarchy

However:

**DO NOT CLONE THE REFERENCE.**

Do not reproduce its exact layout, typography, spacing, navigation, image arrangement, or visual components.

Use it as inspiration for the level of sophistication we want.

Our website should have its own visual identity and storytelling system.

---

# 26. What We Absolutely Do NOT Want

Avoid generic web-design patterns such as:

- Hero with generic stock image + heading + three buttons
- "About Us" immediately after hero
- Six identical service cards
- Rounded rectangular cards everywhere
- Excessive glassmorphism
- Random gradients
- Excessive gold
- Generic luxury fonts
- Huge blocks of text
- Generic testimonial carousel
- Fake statistics
- Random floating blobs
- Excessive shadows
- Overused hover effects
- Random 3D objects
- Excessive border-radius
- Template-looking layouts
- AI-generated-looking copy
- Unnecessary animations
- Animation on every element
- Excessive cursor effects
- Poorly optimized video backgrounds

The site should feel **art-directed**, not assembled from UI components.

---

# 27. Design Principle

The most important principle:

> **Show, don't explain.**

Instead of writing:

"We provide premium interior decoration solutions for modern homeowners."

Show:

A transformation.

Instead of saying:

"We pay attention to detail."

Show:

A close-up of material, texture, stitching, lighting, and craftsmanship.

Instead of saying:

"We create beautiful spaces."

Show:

The finished space.

The website should make the visitor feel the quality rather than convince them through marketing language.

---

# 28. The Experience We Want

When a visitor leaves the website, we want them to remember:

**The atmosphere.**

**The transformation.**

**The spaces.**

**The quality.**

**The feeling.**

Not the number of cards or paragraphs they read.

The ideal reaction is:

> "That didn't feel like a normal business website."

And then:

> "I can actually imagine my home looking like that."

---

# 29. Final Creative Direction

The website should feel like:

**An interior-design magazine**

combined with

**A cinematic walkthrough**

combined with

**A premium brand experience.**

It should communicate the idea that:

> **A home isn't decorated by adding things to a room.  
> It is created by bringing together light, texture, material, form, and the small details that make a space feel yours.**

Every design decision should support this idea.

---

# 30. North Star

If there is ever uncertainty about a design decision, ask:

### Does this make the visitor understand, feel, or experience the transformation of a space?

If yes, consider it.

If it only exists because it is a trendy web-design effect, remove it.

The website should prioritize:

**Story > Visuals > Emotion > Usability > Decoration**

The final product should be modern, memorable, technically polished, and visually ambitious without becoming unnecessarily complicated.

**The website is not just showing homes.

It is showing what a home can become.**
